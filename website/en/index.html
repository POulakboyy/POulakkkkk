Seven modules

One app for your time, tasks and ideas

Each module covers a moment of your day, and they all share the same data: an idea becomes a task, and a task finds its slot in your calendar.

  • Time

    A calendar that rearranges itself for you

    Tasks fill free slots based on how long they take. When a block runs over, the rest of the day shifts without moving fixed appointments.

    • 5 to 15-minute transitions between meetings
    • Natural-language input: “Project meeting Tuesday 2–4pm”
    • Deep-work blocks protected from meetings
  • Tasks

    Execution

    Dependencies, precise recurrence rules and a built-in timer on every task.

    • Single-task focus mode
    • A daily cap on priorities
  • Files

    Local-first storage

    Your data lives on your device and syncs in the background.

    • Encrypted vaults
    • Search by concept
  • Canvas

    Graphs and diagrams

    An infinite canvas to connect ideas, tasks and documents.

    • Automatic layout
    • Minimap and collapsible branches
  • Ideas

    Ideation

    Tools to write without self-censoring and to spark new connections.

    • Instant idea capture
    • Random creative constraints
  • Team

    Collaboration

    Comments on a specific block, polls and a shared inbox.

    • Webhooks and a public API
    • Full JSON export
  • Wellbeing

    Interface and review

    An interface that fades while you work, and a weekly review without blame.

    • Streaks with a skip day
    • E-ink theme

Informed by research

Defaults based on studies, not slogans

We read the studies before choosing a default, and we state how confident we are in each one. Everything stays adjustable.

  • A “ready to resume” note at the end of a block

    Switching away from an unfinished task leaves attention residue that weighs on the next one. Writing one line on where you are and what comes next reduces the effect.

    Leroy, 2009; Leroy & Glomb, 2018Confidence: moderate

  • “When? Where?” for important tasks

    A meta-analysis found that making a specific plan (“when X happens, I’ll do Y”) helps people reach their goals. POuxis suggests a time slot, without forcing one.

    Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006Confidence: strong for the principle

  • Batched notifications

    In one study, checking email three times a day lowered participants’ stress, and holding alerts until a natural break reduces frustration. By default: a digest three times a day, and nothing during a focus block.

    Kushlev & Dunn, 2015; Iqbal & Bailey, 2008Confidence: moderate

  • Forgiving streaks

    A broken streak lowers engagement unless it can be repaired, and missing a single day doesn’t derail a habit in the making. So you get one skip day a week, and no guilt trips.

    Silverman & Barasch, 2022; Lally et al., 2010Confidence: moderate

We also publish what the research doesn’t support. For example, no colour has been shown to “boost” creativity, and 25 minutes isn’t a proven optimal focus length. It’s a convention.

Trust

Your data stays yours

  • Local-first

    Your tasks, notes and canvases are stored on your device. The app works offline with no account, and sync is optional.

  • End-to-end encrypted

    Content is encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before it leaves. The sync relay carries envelopes it can’t read, and only sees what it needs to route them.

  • Open source

    The app is licensed under AGPL-3.0 and the engine under MPL-2.0. You can read and audit the code, and run your own relay.

No ads, no trackers. The same goes for this site: no cookies, no third-party requests. Privacy policy

How it works

Four steps, every day

  1. Capture

    Jot down a task or an idea in one sentence, from any screen. Call Ines Thursday 10am becomes a dated task.

  2. Plan

    POuxis suggests a slot based on the estimated duration and the shape of your day. You accept it, move it, or leave the task floating.

  3. Focus

    A focus block turns on your device’s Do Not Disturb and holds notifications until it ends.

  4. Review

    At the end of the week, compare what you planned with what got done: wins first, then what held you up.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When will POuxis be available?

POuxis is in development. The first test builds will be published on GitHub, then through TestFlight and the app stores’ testing tracks. Watch the repository to hear about them.

Do I need an account?

No. POuxis runs on your device without an account. Syncing between your devices is optional.

Can the sync server read my notes?

No. Content is encrypted on your device with a key the relay never holds. The relay sees routing metadata only: the space ID, a random device ID, a logical clock and the approximate size of each envelope. Like any server, it also sees your IP address while connected; it doesn't log it.

Can I host my own relay?

Yes. The sync relay is part of the repository and runs on Node.js. It’s published under AGPL-3.0.

Does POuxis promise to make me more productive?

No. We use research to choose sensible defaults, and we publish how confident we are in each choice. Effects vary from person to person, and everything stays adjustable.

How can I contribute?

Open an issue or a pull request on GitHub. Contributions are signed off with Signed-off-by (DCO), with no copyright assignment.
